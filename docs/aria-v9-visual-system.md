# ARIA V9 Visual System

## Product position

ARIA is a feminine project operator and creative companion, not a cartoon avatar and not a claim of literal consciousness. Her visual identity should feel composed, intelligent, warm, and technically capable. The product family should look like one considered organization with distinct modes: a focused operator console, an editorial anime catalog, and a calm learning space.

## Brand mark

The mark is a simple orbital-flower signal: a graceful feminine A-shaped negative space formed by two tapered petals and a small orbit point. It must work in one color, at 20px, and as a square app icon. Avoid faces, humanoid characters, metallic gamer emblems, shields, crowns, mascots, excessive gradients, illegible generated wordmarks, and masculine esports geometry. The production lockup uses the mark plus the word ARIA; exact typography remains code-rendered rather than generated inside the image.

## Surface rules

The dashboard uses high-information density with a quiet canvas, one strong page title, section labels, a compact left rail on desktop, and a bottom navigation/compact drawer pattern on small screens. The anime catalog uses image-led editorial cards, restrained badges, large readable titles, and a clear streaming/download action hierarchy. The learner portal uses calm surfaces, short copy blocks, visible progress metrics, and one primary next action per view. Pairing uses a centered trust-first card with clear security status and a large QR region.

Across all surfaces, hierarchy must not depend on color alone. Focus states must be visible, controls must meet touch-size expectations, copy must be left-aligned and scannable, and responsive layouts must avoid horizontal scrolling. Motion is reserved for gentle state transitions and not used as decoration around primary content.

## Candidate review

Candidate A has a strong A-like orbital silhouette but reads too much like a pointed gaming crest and contains visible green generation contamination. Candidate B is more feminine and flower-like, but it is too literal, too busy at small sizes, and also contains visible green artifacts. Neither candidate is suitable as a production logo without a focused regeneration; the next mark should be flatter, more minimal, cleaner, and tested at favicon scale.

Candidate C is cleaner and clearly communicates an A, but its three-lobed silhouette is still close to a generic flower and the generated background-removal edge is not yet production-clean. Candidate D is the strongest direction so far: it reads as a graceful A-shaped orbital ribbon with a clear signal dot and a more distinctive feminine motion. It still needs a clean crop/background treatment and a tiny-size check before adoption.

Candidate E is compact and softer than C, but its open central form is less immediately recognizable as ARIA than D. The production direction is therefore based on D: a restrained orbital A-ribbon with a coral signal node. Before integration, the asset will be cleaned to remove the warm background/edge halo, cropped to a balanced square, and exported as a small web asset plus a larger source mark.

The cleaned D-direction mark is accepted as the V9 production direction. It has a distinctly feminine, graceful A-ribbon silhouette, a restrained coral signal dot, no generated lettering, and no visible green contamination. The preview presents the intended transparency checkerboard; the next integration step will copy it into the shared public asset path and pair it with code-rendered ARIA typography.
